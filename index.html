Marcos
Skowronek Santos

I am a PhD student in the Department of Physics at Brown University. My research explores the mathematical structures of scattering amplitudes and their connections to geometry, cosmology, and gravity.

My interests range from positive geometry and surfaceology to the singularity structure of observables and amplitude methods for classical and semiclassical gravity.

Before joining Brown in 2023, I obtained my M.Sc. in Physics at the University of Copenhagen and my B.A. in Physics at Universidad Complutense de Madrid.

Research interests

01

Positive geometries and surfaceology

I study geometric and combinatorial descriptions of scattering amplitudes in quantum field theory and string theory, which make physical properties such as locality and unitarity manifest, and reveal deep hidden connections between different theories.

02

Singularity structure for scattering amplitudes

I investigate how the singularities of on-shell observables can be used to constrain or reconstruct them. This approach can be applied to a wide range of objects, from Wilson loops to cosmological correlators.

03

Observables in gravity from amplitudes

I use scattering amplitudes to study classical and semiclassical observables in gravity. My interests include spinning bodies, bound states, and quantum corrections, with the aim of understanding how scattering data encodes gravitational dynamics.
